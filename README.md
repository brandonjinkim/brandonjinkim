## Joko

As part of my Capstone project at the University of Washington, my student group and I are collaborating with the team at Joko to create their fan and artist interface and backend system, utilizing TypeScript and React for the frontend and TypeScript, Express, Node.js for the backend.

### The Problem

Independent artists face an increasingly unstable and inequitable music economy where the vast majority struggle to earn sustainable income despite growing global audiences. While streaming platforms have democratized access to music, they disproportionately reward the top 1% of artists, who capture 90% of all revenue, leaving the remaining 99% to fight for a 10% share. This disparity is even more pronounced for artists in the Global South, particularly in Nigeria where JOKO was founded, as they must navigate a fragmented and high-cost financial landscape to access their international earnings.

The current financial system forces these creators to rely on legacy banking infrastructure and correspondent banking networks that are fundamentally ill-suited for the modern digital economy. For an artist in Africa to receive international streaming royalties or fan payments, funds must navigate a fragmented landscape of 40+ different currencies, often undergoing "double currency conversions" and costly offshore clearing in USD or EUR.

These inefficiencies hit independent artists the hardest:

Highest Global Fees: While the artist's thin margins are already squeezed by streaming platforms, formal remittance fees in the region average between 7.4% and 8.3%—the highest in the world.

The "Middleman Tax": Because most local banks lack direct international relationships, they must use intermediary correspondent banks that add their own layers of fees (ranging from $20 to $100 per transaction) and significant delays.

Settlement Delays: While an artist might need immediate funds for touring or production, traditional bank-led settlement times can range from 1 to 5 days, compared to the near-instant potential of digital-first solutions.

### The Design Challenge

How might we design a direct artist-to-fan subscription experience that bypasses predatory streaming models and fragmented payment rails to enable sustainable, cross-border income, foster meaningful fan relationships, and empower Global South artists to maintain total ownership over their financial and creative futures?
